Improving medical research summarization with domain-adapted AI

Dec 11, 2025 · 4 min read

We are pleased to share that our research paper, Domain Adaptation for Multi‑document Summarisation: A Case Study in the Medical Research Domain, has been accepted and presented at the Pacific Asia Conference on Language, Information and Computation (PACLIC) 2025, the 39th edition of an established international conference focused on language, information, and computational linguistics. The conference proceedings are published in the ACL Anthology, reflecting its place within the broader computational linguistics and natural language processing research community.

The research focuses on a practical challenge in medical research: how can AI help make the growing volume of scientific literature easier to understand without losing important details? Medical research papers can be lengthy and highly technical, making it difficult to efficiently review large numbers of studies and identify their key findings. In this work, we explored how an existing AI summarization model can be adapted specifically to the medical domain, with the goal of producing concise summaries that better preserve important information and factual details. This could help researchers, healthcare professionals, and policymakers process large volumes of medical literature more efficiently.

A key contribution of this work is the domain adaptation of PRIMERA, a model designed for multi‑document summarization, to the medical research domain using the MSˆ2 dataset. Fine‑tuning led to substantial improvements in summarization performance, with relative gains of approximately 159% to 225% across the three ROUGE metrics. The adapted model also consistently outperformed the open‑source large language models evaluated in zero‑shot settings, including LLaMA 3.2 3B, Mistral 7B, OpenChat 7B, and Gemma 7B. To assess the quality of the generated summaries more comprehensively, we combined automated metrics with evaluations by medical experts and an LLM‑based judge, providing evidence of stronger factual accuracy, coverage, and domain relevance.

Beyond medical summarization, this work demonstrates the broader value of adapting AI models to specific domains rather than relying solely on general‑purpose models. While general‑purpose AI offers flexibility across many tasks, specialized models can provide important advantages when accuracy, domain knowledge, and resource efficiency are particularly important. The approach explored in this research could potentially be applied to other specialized areas where large volumes of complex information need to be processed reliably.

This marks the fourth publication resulting from our ongoing collaboration with the Department of Computer Science & Engineering at the University of Moratuwa. The paper was authored by Kushan Hewapathirana, a member of the ConscientAI team who was also a postgraduate student at the Department of Computer Science & Engineering, University of Moratuwa, Nisansa de Silva, Senior Lecturer at the department, C.D. Athuraliya from ConscientAI, and Piumi Kandanaarachchi, a Senior Resident Medical Officer at District General Hospital, Hambantota. The involvement of a medical doctor in this research reflects the value of combining technical and domain expertise when developing AI for specialized fields.
